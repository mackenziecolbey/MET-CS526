Problem 1 (35 pts) - Snowfall
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

You are given an array A[1…n] which contains the cumulative snowfall totals for
n consecutive days, so A[i] is the total snowfall from day 1 through day i.
For example, if there are 6 inches of snow on day 1, 7 inches of snow on
day 2, 0 inches of snow on day 3 and 4 inches of snow on day 4, the array
would contain the values [6,13,13,17].

The input file contains 2 lines: the first line is the number of days n and
the second line is the space separated list of the n cumulative snowfall
totals.

Design and implement an algorithm that determines if there exist three
consecutive days whose combined snowfall is strictly more than half of the
total snowfall across all n days (the total is A[n]).

Notes:
  • Any three consecutive days may be used, including days 1, 2 and 3. The
    snowfall on days i, i+1 and i+2 is A[i+2] - A[i-1], where A[0] = 0.
  • "More than half" is strict: if the three days produce exactly half of the
    total, the answer for that window is NO.
  • If n is less than 3, the answer is NO.

Your solution should return either YES or NO.

You should demonstrate your code by running it on each of the four sample
inputs below. You may run all of the inputs in a single execution of your code
or individually. For each input, your output should be the input values you
are testing followed by your solution, in the form:

    <input> solution: <YES|NO>

  • Describe the heart of your algorithm
  • Write a program that takes as input a file and prints to the console
    "<input> solution: <YES|NO>"
  • Provide a readme.txt which explains how to run your code in a terminal
    window.

Note: there are four sample inputs (snowfall_input1.txt - snowfall_input4.txt):

8
1 4 10 14 15 17 17 23

16
44 127 166 231 238 320 363 429 500 511 577 642 689 764 812 886

9
3 8 14 19 21 24 25 25 32

5
1 2 3 4 5

Results:

1 4 10 14 15 17 17 23 solution: YES
44 127 166 231 238 320 363 429 500 511 577 642 689 764 812 886 solution: NO
3 8 14 19 21 24 25 25 32 solution: NO
1 2 3 4 5 solution: YES

Problem 2 (35 pts) - Pandemic
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

You are in a square state whose counties are arranged into an NxN grid. Your
code must work for any N; the sample inputs use N = 5 (25 counties). Two
counties are neighbors if they are horizontally or vertically next to each
other (diagonal counties are not neighbors).

e.g.

  . . . . .
  . A B . .
  . . C . .
  . . . . .
  . . . . .

A and B are neighbors. B and C are neighbors. A and C are not neighbors.

Some of the counties are infected by a mysterious virus which is highly
contagious. Once a county is infected it stays infected. Each day, every
healthy county that has at least two infected neighbors becomes infected.
All counties are updated at the same time: a county infected today does not
count as an infected neighbor until the next day.

For example, let's start with the following configuration of infected
counties (marked X). Day 1 is the initial state:

Day 1               Day 2               Day 3
  . . . . .           . . . . .           . . . . .
  . . . X .           . . X X .           . . X X .
  . . X . .           . . X X .           . . X X X
  . . . . X           . . . . X           . . . X X
  . . . . .           . . . . .           . . . . .

Day 4               Day 5 – no new counties are infected, so the
  . . . . .                 infection has stopped spreading
  . . X X X           . . X X X
  . . X X X           . . X X X
  . . X X X           . . X X X
  . . . . .           . . . . .

Write an algorithm that simulates the infection until it stops spreading and
then determines whether there are any healthy counties left in the state.

You will be given 2 input files which have the form:
  Line 1: N, the size of the matrix
  Each following line: the row and column of one initially infected county,
  separated by a space

Rows and columns are numbered starting at 0, with row 0 at the top and
column 0 at the left.

e.g.
5
1 3
2 2
3 4

This input means that the matrix is 5x5 and the counties at (row 1, column 3),
(row 2, column 2) and (row 3, column 4) are initially infected. This is the
Day 1 configuration shown above.

  • Describe the heart of your algorithm
  • Write a program that takes as input a file and prints to the console
    either "There are healthy counties left" or
    "There are no healthy counties left"
  • You may also print the state of the board for each day, but the final
    line of output must be one of the two messages above.
  • Provide a readme.txt which explains how to run your code in a terminal
    window.

Note: there are two sample inputs:
    pandemic_input1.txt -> There are healthy counties left
    pandemic_input2.txt -> There are no healthy counties left

Problem 3 (30 pts) - Symbol Puzzle Game
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

You are given an nxn puzzle board where the square root of n is an integer,
e.g. 4x4 or 9x9. The board is populated with symbols, where the number of
distinct symbols is equal to n.

Example: A partially filled 4x4 puzzle board using the symbols @ # & * with
a . representing an empty cell:

  @ . . .
  . * . #
  # . * &
  * & . @

Create and code an algorithm to calculate if the current board is in a valid
or invalid state. The board is valid if all of the following conditions are
met:

  • Each row contains each symbol at most once
  • Each column contains each symbol at most once
  • The board is divided into n non-overlapping sub-boards, each of size
    √n x √n (for a 4x4 board, four 2x2 sub-boards; for a 9x9 board, nine
    3x3 sub-boards), starting from the top-left corner. Each sub-board
    contains each symbol at most once.
  • Every non-empty cell contains one of the n listed symbols. A cell holding
    any other symbol makes the board invalid.

Empty cells (.) are allowed anywhere and never cause the board to be invalid.

You will be provided 2 input files with the following structure:
  Line 1: n
  Line 2: the n symbols, separated by commas (the commas are separators and
          are not symbols)
  Lines 3 to n+2: the n rows of the board, each containing n comma separated
          values, where each value is a symbol or a . for an empty cell

e.g. (this is the example board above, which is valid)

4
@,*,&,#
@,.,.,.
.,*,.,#
#,.,*,&
*,&,.,@

  • Describe the heart of your algorithm
  • Write a program that takes as input a file and prints to the console
    either "The board is valid" or "The board is invalid"
  • Provide a readme.txt which explains how to run your code in a terminal
    window.

Note: there are two sample inputs:
    puzzle_game_input1.txt -> The board is valid
    puzzle_game_input2.txt -> The board is invalid
